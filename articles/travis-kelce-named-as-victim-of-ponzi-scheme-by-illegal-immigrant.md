Kansas City Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce has been identified as one of dozens of victims in a multimillion‑dollar Ponzi scheme run by Texas investor Siddharth Jawahar, according to federal prosecutors.

Jawahar, founder of Swiftarc Capital LLC, was sentenced to 11 years in federal prison after pleading guilty in January to three counts of wire fraud. Prosecutors say he defrauded Kelce and 63 other investors by falsely claiming their money was being placed into profitable ventures.

The case began in 2023, when a federal grand jury indicted Jawahar following an investigation into Swiftarc’s finances. According to the Department of Justice, Jawahar told investors their funds were being used to support specific companies and that Swiftarc was generating strong returns. In reality, prosecutors say no legitimate investing was taking place.

A Ponzi scheme is a form of financial fraud in which a company pays earlier investors using money collected from newer ones. This creates the illusion of profit and stability, encouraging more people to invest. Because no real revenue is generated, the scheme eventually collapses when the operator can no longer attract new funds.

Federal officials say Jawahar used investor funds for personal luxury travel, including private jet flights and stays at high‑end hotels in Austin and New York. Prosecutors also allege he asked a relative to wipe his phone in an attempt to conceal evidence during the investigation.

Jawahar managed more than a dozen affiliated companies, all of which prosecutors say were tied to the fraudulent operation.

A U.S. District Court judge handed down the 11‑year sentence this week. As of now, Kelce has not publicly commented on being named among the victims.
