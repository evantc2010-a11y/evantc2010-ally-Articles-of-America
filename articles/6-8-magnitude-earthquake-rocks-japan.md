A 6.8 magnitude earthquake has shaken Japan, striking the country’s southern main island of Kyushu late Tuesday afternoon. 

A dozen fires have broken out, and Japanese media outlet NHK is reporting 50 injuries, according to FOX Weather. 

A tsunami warning was issued, though no tsunami has been detected.

In terms of damage, a shopping mall has collapsed, and officials fear people may be trapped inside. The Kyushu Electric Power Company reports that nearly 46,000 homes in Kumamoto Prefecture have lost power.

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has posted instructions on 𝕏 in response to the earthquake, emphasizing the need to prioritize human life above all and to provide accurate information to the public.

Japan sits on the Ring of Fire, making the country no stranger to earthquakes and tsunamis. The most deadly disaster occurred in 2011, claiming nearly 20,000 lives and injuring another 6,200.The city of Kumamoto was also hit by a major earthquake in 2016, which caused 278 deaths.
